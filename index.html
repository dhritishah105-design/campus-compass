Transport

Getting around without losing a whole day

The local train, the metro, buses and autos — what each one is actually for, and the things people only tell you after you have already got it wrong.

Fares, timings and metro lines change regularly. Check m-Indicator or the operator before you travel — nothing on this page is a current price.

The local train

The spine of the city. Vile Parle is on the Western Line, and Andheri is the big junction just north of it.

How the three lines fit together

Things to know

  • Know your direction, not just your stationTrains are announced by their final destination. Churchgate is south towards town, Virar and Borivali are north towards the suburbs. Get the direction wrong and you will know within one stop.
  • Fast trains skip the small stationsVile Parle is a slow-train stop. A fast train will sail straight past it and leave you at Andheri or Bandra. Check the board before you board.
  • Rush hour has a directionMornings are packed heading south towards town, evenings heading north towards the suburbs. Travelling against the flow is a completely different experience.
  • First class existsIt costs considerably more than second class and is noticeably less crowded. Worth it on days you cannot face the crush.
  • Ladies’ compartments are reservedThey are marked on the platform and on the coach. Men travelling in them can be fined, and the rules are enforced.
  • Stand at the right end of the platformWork out which end your exit is at and board that coach. It saves you fighting the length of the platform at the other end.
  • Never ride the footboardPeople are killed on this line every year doing exactly that. If a train is too full, wait for the next one. Another comes in minutes.

Booking your tickets

  • RailOne is the app now, not UTSThe old UTS app was retired on 1 March 2026 and RailOne replaced it for unreserved and suburban tickets. Same job: buy on your phone instead of queueing. You have to be physically near the station for it to let you book.
  • A season pass beats buying each timeA monthly or quarterly pass works out far cheaper than a ticket a day, and you walk straight past the queue. Worth it from the first week if you travel most days.
  • Ask about the student concession earlyStudents can get a concession pass with a certificate from college. The paperwork takes time, so start it before term gets busy.
  • Always travel with a valid ticketCheckers do spot checks and the penalty is far more than the fare. If you bought a pass in the old UTS app, keep a screenshot until it expires — old bookings did not carry over.

No timings on this page on purpose. They change, and m-Indicator has the live ones along with the full station map.

Metro and buses

Slower than the local in a straight line, but air-conditioned, far less crowded, and much better for east–west trips the train does not make.

The lines worth knowing

  • Line 1 · BlueVersova – Andheri – Ghatkopar. The east–west one, and the most useful from campus. Andheri is where it meets the Western line.
  • Line 2A · YellowDahisar to Andheri West, running up the western suburbs parallel to the train.
  • Line 7 · RedDahisar East to Gundavali in Andheri East. The eastern twin of the Yellow line.
  • Line 3 · AquaUnderground, Aarey through BKC down to Cuffe Parade. This is the one that reaches BKC and south Mumbai without the road traffic.

The network keeps growing and new sections open every year. Check a current map rather than one a senior sent you last year.

Things to know

  • Andheri is the meeting pointIt is where the metro, the Western line and the Harbour branch all come together. Most metro journeys from campus start by getting to Andheri.
  • It closes earlier than the localDo not plan a late night around catching the metro home. Check the last service before you go out.
  • Tickets at the counter or the machineBuy a token or tap a card at the gate. Card and app options exist and differ by line, so check what the line you use accepts.
  • Worth it in MayIn peak summer and in the monsoon, the air conditioning is not a luxury. Many people switch to metro for those months and back to the train after.

Buses

  • BEST buses fill the gapsFor the short hops the train and metro do not cover, buses go almost everywhere and cost very little.
  • Chalo shows live busesThe Chalo app tracks buses in real time and sells passes, which beats standing at a stop guessing.

Autos, cabs and bikes

For everything the rail network does not reach, which is most of your daily life around campus.

Which one to take

  • Short hop around Vile Parle, Juhu or AndheriAn auto on the meter. Almost always the fastest and cheapest for a few kilometres.
  • Anywhere south of Bandra or MahimAutos are not allowed down there. It is a black-and-yellow taxi or an app cab.
  • Station to a common destinationA share auto from the stand. Fixed route, shared with others, a fraction of the price.
  • Late at night, or aloneAn app cab with the trip shared to someone. The tracking is the point, not the comfort.

Things to know

  • Insist on the meterFor autos in the suburbs the meter is the norm. Agreeing a price up front almost always costs you more.
  • App cabs surge exactly when you need themRain and rush hour are when prices climb and cars disappear. On a wet evening an auto or the train will usually beat waiting for a cab.
  • Bike taxi rules keep changingThey have been permitted, banned and re-permitted in Maharashtra more than once. Check what is currently legal before relying on one.
  • Share autos are not obvious to findThey run from fixed points near stations. Ask at the stand which ones go your way — nobody will tell you unprompted.

Paying

  • UPI works almost everywhereMost autos and taxis now take it. Keep a little cash anyway for the ones that do not, and for when the network drops.
  • Round up, do not haggle over ten rupeesStandard practice, and it makes the next ride easier for everyone.

Planning around the city

  • Sunday megablocks are realPlanned maintenance shuts stretches of line, usually on Sundays. m-Indicator flags them. Check before committing to a Sunday plan across town.
  • Leave a buffer in the monsoonA journey that takes forty minutes in January can take two hours in July. Build it in rather than apologising later.
  • Screenshot your passPhone batteries die and station signal is patchy. A screenshot has saved a lot of people a fine.

Mumbai basics

Things nobody tells you until it is too late

The monsoon, the hours the city keeps, the paperwork, and staying safe. Written by students who learned most of this the hard way.

This is general advice from student experience, not official guidance. Check anything time-sensitive or legal for yourself.

The monsoon

Roughly June to September. It is not drizzle, and it reshapes how the whole city works for four months.

  • Waterlogging stops the city, not just youLow-lying stretches flood, trains slow or stop, and roads gridlock. On a heavy day, check before you set out rather than after.
  • Do not walk through standing waterOpen manholes are a genuine hazard, and the city issues leptospirosis warnings every year after people wade through floodwater with cuts on their feet.
  • Waterproof your bag, not just yourselfAn umbrella keeps your head dry and nothing else. A dry bag or a liner for your laptop matters more than a raincoat.
  • Buy footwear you are willing to destroyWhatever you wear in the monsoon will be ruined by September. Everyone has a pair for the season.
  • Keep a buffer on heavy daysCharged phone, something to eat, and the expectation that a journey may take three times as long. Some days you simply stay where you are.

How the week runs

The city keeps its own hours, and learning them early saves a lot of wasted journeys.

  • Dinner is lateEating at nine or ten at night is completely normal, and plenty of places serve well past midnight. Nobody will think it is odd.
  • Crossing town at six in the evening is a mistakeIf you can shift a journey by an hour either side of the evening peak, do. The difference is enormous.
  • Sundays can mean engineering worksMegablocks take out stretches of line for maintenance. Check before planning a Sunday that depends on the train.
  • Festivals close roadsGanesh Chaturthi in particular brings processions that shut major routes for hours. Look up the dates at the start of term and plan around them.
  • Shops have their own weekly day offIt varies by area and trade. The shop that is shut today is not shut permanently.

Money and admin

Nothing here is a price. Costs move, and you should check current numbers yourself.

  • Set up UPI in your first weekIt is accepted almost everywhere, down to small stalls and autos. It will be how you pay for most things.
  • Still carry some cashConnections drop, and some small vendors stay cash-only. A little cash for autos and chai saves awkwardness.
  • Track your first month properlyMost people are surprised by where the money actually goes here, and it is rarely the obvious things. Knowing your own number makes the rest of the year easier.
  • Agree flatmate splits in writingRent, electricity, internet, water, cook, cleaning. A note in a group chat at the start prevents the argument in month four.
  • Keep digital copies of your documentsID, admission letter, agreement, photos. You will be asked for them more often than you expect.

Staying safe

Mumbai is comparatively easy to move around in late, but the ordinary precautions still apply.

  • Share your trip when travelling alone at nightLive location to one person who will notice. It costs nothing and it is the single most useful habit.
  • 112 is the national emergency number100 reaches the police. Save your local police station and your college emergency contact in your phone as well.
  • Carry your college IDIt identifies you, it gets you student rates in some places, and it matters if anything goes wrong.
  • Never cross the tracksThere are bridges at every station. Crossing the rails kills people on this network every year, and it is not worth the three minutes.
  • Trust the crowdBusy, well-lit and populated is safer than the shortcut. In this city there is almost always a busier route.

The first few weeks

The admin nobody warns you about, roughly in the order you will need it.

  • Get a local SIM earlyYou will need ID and proof of address. Almost everything else, from UPI to deliveries, depends on having a working local number.
  • Expect police verification on a rentalVerifying tenants is standard practice here and your landlord should be arranging it. It is normal, not a red flag.
  • Agreements usually run eleven monthsThat is the common arrangement for rentals. Read what you sign, and keep your own copy of it.
  • Brokerage is usually quoted as one month’s rentConfirm the figure and what it covers before you agree to anything, and get it in writing.
  • Know your nearest hospital before you need oneFind it in week one, not at two in the morning in your first monsoon.

About us

Built by a Mumbaikar who wanted to make student life easier

Having grown up in Mumbai, I knew the city well, but watching my classmates navigate student life here made me realise how overwhelming it could be. From finding affordable places to eat and reliable study spots to figuring out everyday student essentials, there was a lot of information scattered around, and not always someone to point you in the right direction.

I hope this little initiative makes student life in Mumbai a little easier by bringing helpful information, local recommendations and useful resources together in one place. If it saves someone time, helps them discover a new favourite spot or makes their college experience just a little smoother, it has served its purpose.
